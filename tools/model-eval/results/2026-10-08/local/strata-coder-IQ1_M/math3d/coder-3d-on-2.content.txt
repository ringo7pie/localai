Let

\[
S_1=OA,\qquad S_2=AB,\qquad S_3=BC
\]

and \(V_i=\{X;\,d(X,S_i)\le r\}\), where \(d\) is Euclidean distance.  
Throughout, \(1/2<r<1\).

---

## (1) 平面 \(y=t\) と \(V_1,V_3\) の共通点条件

### 共通点条件

平面 \(y=t\) 与 \(V_1\) の共通点 exists iff

\[
|t|\le r.
\]

平面 \(y=t\) 与 \(V_3\) の共通点 exists iff

\[
|1-t|\le r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1-r\le t\le 1+r.
\]

Since \(1/2<r<1\),

\[
[-r,r]\cap[1-r,1+r]=[1-r,r].
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\,1-r\le t\le r\,}.
\]

---

### cross-section diagrams in the plane \(y=t\)

Put coordinates \((x,z)\) on the plane \(y=t\), with \(x\) horizontal and \(z\) vertical.

Define

\[
a=\sqrt{r^2-t^2},\qquad b=\sqrt{r^2-(1-t)^2}.
\]

For \(t\in[1-r,r]\), both \(a,b\ge0\).

#### \(V_1\) の cross-section

The centerline of \(V_1\) intersects the plane \(y=t\) as the horizontal segment

\[
(0,0)\text{--}(1,0).
\]

Thus

\[
V_1\cap\{y=t\}
\]

is the 2D capsule whose axis is this segment and whose radius is \(a\):

\[
V_1\cap\{y=t\}
=
\{(x,z);\ \mathrm{dist}((x,z),[0,1]\times\{0\})\le a\}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
V_1\cap\{y=t\}
=
([0,1]\times[-a,a])
\ \cup\ 
D((0,0),a)
\ \cup\ 
D((1,0),a),
\]

where \(D(P,\rho)\) is the closed disk centered at \(P\) with radius \(\rho\).

#### \(V_3\) の cross-section

The centerline of \(V_3\) intersects the plane \(y=t\) as the vertical segment

\[
(1,0)\text{--}(1,1).
\]

Thus

\[
V_3\cap\{y=t\}
=
\{(x,z);\ \mathrm{dist}((x,z),\{1\}\times[0,1])\le b\}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
V_3\cap\{y=t\}
=
([1-b,1+b]\times[0,1])
\ \cup\ 
D((1,0),b)
\ \cup\ 
D((1,1),b).
\]

#### Desired figure

On the same \((x,z)\)-plane, draw the L-shaped unit segment

\[
(0,0)\to(1,0)\to(1,1),
\]

then thicken the horizontal part by radius \(a\) and the vertical part by radius \(b\).  
The two capsule-shaped cross-sections meet at the point \((1,0)\). At the endpoints \(t=1-r\) or \(t=r\), one of the radii becomes \(0\), so the corresponding capsule degenerates into a line segment.

---

## (2) Condition for \(V_1\cap V_3\subset V_2\)

We claim that

\[
\boxed{\,r\le \frac1{\sqrt2}\,}
\]

is necessary and sufficient.

Because the problem assumes \(1/2<r<1\), the final condition is

\[
\boxed{\,\frac12<r\le \frac1{\sqrt2}\,}.
\]

---

### Sufficiency

Take any point \(X=(x,y,z)\in V_1\cap V_3\).

Since \(d(X,S_1)\le r\) and \(d(X,S_3)\le r\), the \(y\)-coordinate must satisfy

\[
|y|\le r,\qquad |1-y|\le r.
\]

Thus

\[
y\in[1-r,r]\subset[0,1].
\]

Also, from \(d(X,S_3)\le r\),

\[
|1-x|\le r,
\]

and since \(r<1\),

\[
x\ge 1-r>0.
\]

We check \(X\in V_2\) by cases.

---

### Case 1: \(x\ge1\)

For \(V_1\),

\[
d(X,S_1)^2=(x-1)^2+y^2+z^2\le r^2.
\]

Since \(y\in[0,1]\), the \(y\)-distance to \(S_2=AB\) is zero, so

\[
d(X,S_2)^2=(x-1)^2+z^2
\le (x-1)^2+y^2+z^2
\le r^2.
\]

Hence \(X\in V_2\).

---

### Case 2: \(0<x\le1\)

Here \(x\in[0,1]\). Also \(z\in[-r,r]\), so \(|z|<1\).

#### Subcase 2-1: \(z\le0\)

For \(V_3\),

\[
d(X,S_3)^2=(1-x)^2+(1-y)^2+z^2\le r^2.
\]

Again \(y\in[0,1]\), so

\[
d(X,S_2)^2=(1-x)^2+z^2
\le (1-x)^2+(1-y)^2+z^2
\le r^2.
\]

Thus \(X\in V_2\).

#### Subcase 2-2: \(0\le z\)

Since \(z<r<1\), \(z\in[0,1]\). Then

\[
d(X,S_1)^2=y^2+z^2\le r^2,
\]

and

\[
d(X,S_3)^2=(1-x)^2+(1-y)^2\le r^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
(1-x)^2\le r^2-(1-y)^2,
\qquad
z^2\le r^2-y^2.
\]

Hence

\[
(1-x)^2+z^2
\le
2r^2-\{y^2+(1-y)^2\}.
\]

But for \(0\le y\le1\),

\[
y^2+(1-y)^2\ge \frac12.
\]

Therefore, if \(r^2\le1/2\),

\[
d(X,S_2)^2
=
(1-x)^2+z^2
\le
2r^2-\frac12
\le r^2.
\]

So \(X\in V_2\).

Thus, if \(r\le1/\sqrt2\), then

\[
V_1\cap V_3\subset V_2.
\]

---

### Necessity

Assume

\[
r>\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]

Let

\[
s=\sqrt{r^2-\frac14}.
\]

Because \(r<1\), we have \(0<s<1\). Consider

\[
X=(1-s,\frac12,s).
\]

Then \(x=1-s\in[0,1]\), \(y=1/2\in[0,1]\), \(z=s\in[0,1]\). We compute squared distances.

For \(S_1=OA\),

\[
d(X,S_1)^2=y^2+z^2
=\frac14+s^2=r^2.
\]

For \(S_3=BC\),

\[
d(X,S_3)^2=(1-x)^2+(1-y)^2
=s^2+\frac14=r^2.
\]

So \(X\in V_1\cap V_3\).

But for \(S_2=AB\),

\[
d(X,S_2)^2=(1-x)^2+z^2
=s^2+s^2
=2r^2-\frac12.
\]

Since \(r^2>1/2\),

\[
2r^2-\frac12>r^2.
\]

Thus \(X\notin V_2\). Hence inclusion fails for \(r>1/\sqrt2\). Therefore the condition is also necessary.

## (3) Volume of \(V_1\)

\(V_1\) is the solid swept by a sphere of radius \(r\) whose center moves along a unit segment \(OA\). This is a cylinder of radius \(r\) and height \(1\), together with two hemispheres of radius \(r\), i.e. a sphere.

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1)
=
\pi r^2\cdot1+\frac43\pi r^3.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1)
=
\pi r^2+\frac43\pi r^3
}.
\]

Let us denote this volume by

\[
S=\pi r^2+\frac43\pi r^3.
\]

By the same reason,

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1)=\operatorname{Vol}(V_2)=\operatorname{Vol}(V_3)=S.
\]

---

## (4) Volume of \(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3\) under the condition of (2)

First compute the volume of an adjacent pair intersection, for example

\[
J=\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_2).
\]

By an isometry, we may regard \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) as the \(r\)-neighborhoods of the two unit segments

\[
S_x=\{(u,0,0);\ 0\le u\le1\},
\qquad
S_y=\{(0,v,0);\ 0\le v\le1\}
\]

meeting at right angle at the origin.

Because \(r<1\), the far endpoints \(u=1\), \(v=1\) do not affect the intersection.

Fix \(z\in[-r,r]\), and put

\[
s=\sqrt{r^2-z^2}.
\]

In the \(xy\)-plane at height \(z\), the intersection \(V_1\cap V_2\) has the following cross-section:

- In the first quadrant \(x\ge0,\ y\ge0\), the conditions are

\[
y^2\le s^2,\qquad x^2\le s^2,
\]

so the region is a square of side \(s\), area \(s^2\).

- In the other three quadrants, at least one of \(x,y\) is negative, so the common endpoint \((0,0,0)\) controls the distance. The region is a quarter-disk of radius \(s\) in each of these three quadrants.

Therefore the cross-section area at height \(z\) is

\[
A(z)=s^2+3\cdot\frac{\pi s^2}{4}
=
\left(1+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)s^2
=
\left(1+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)(r^2-z^2).
\]

Thus

\[
J
=
\int_{-r}^r A(z)\,dz
=
\left(1+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)
\int_{-r}^r (r^2-z^2)\,dz.
\]

Now

\[
\int_{-r}^r (r^2-z^2)\,dz
=
\frac43 r^3.
\]

So

\[
J
=
\left(1+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)\frac43 r^3
=
\pi r^3+\frac43 r^3
=
\left(\pi+\frac43\right)r^3.
\]

By symmetry,

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_2)
=
\operatorname{Vol}(V_2\cap V_3)
=
J
=
\left(\pi+\frac43\right)r^3.
\]

Now assume the condition from (2), namely

\[
V_1\cap V_3\subset V_2.
\]

Then the two sets \(V_1\setminus V_2\) and \(V_3\setminus V_2\) are disjoint: if a point belonged to both, it would be in \(V_1\cap V_3\) but outside \(V_2\), contradicting the condition.

Therefore, under the condition,

\[
V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3
=
V_2
\ \cup\ 
(V_1\setminus V_2)
\ \cup\ 
(V_3\setminus V_2)
\]

is a disjoint decomposition. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
\operatorname{Vol}(V_2)
+
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\setminus V_2)
+
\operatorname{Vol}(V_3\setminus V_2).
\]

Since

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\setminus V_2)=S-J,
\qquad
\operatorname{Vol}(V_3\setminus V_2)=S-J,
\]

we get

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
S+(S-J)+(S-J)
=
3S-2J.
\]

Substitute \(S\) and \(J\):

\[
3S-2J
=
3\left(\pi r^2+\frac43\pi r^3\right)
-
2\left(\pi r^3+\frac43 r^3\right).
\]

Simplifying,

\[
3S-2J
=
3\pi r^2
+
\left(2\pi-\frac83\right)r^3.
\]

Therefore, when

\[
\frac12<r\le\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]

the required volume is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
3\pi r^2+\left(2\pi-\frac83\right)r^3
}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
\pi r^2(3+2r)-\frac83r^3
}.
\]
