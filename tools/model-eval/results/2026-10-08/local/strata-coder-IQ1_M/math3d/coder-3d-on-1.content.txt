Let  
\[
S_1=OA,\quad S_2=AB,\quad S_3=BC,
\]
and write \(B_r\) for the closed ball of radius \(r\). Then the swept solids are the \(r\)-neighborhoods of the segments:
\[
V_i=S_i+B_r.
\]

---

## (1)

### Range of \(t\)

For \(V_1\), the segment \(OA\) has \(y=0\), so the plane \(y=t\) meets \(V_1\) iff
\[
-t\le t\le r\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad -r\le t\le r.
\]

For \(V_3\), the segment \(BC\) has \(y=1\), so the plane \(y=t\) meets \(V_3\) iff
\[
|t-1|\le r\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad 1-r\le t\le 1+r.
\]

Because \(\frac12<r<1\),
\[
1-r<r.
\]
Hence the required range is

\[
\boxed{\,1-r\le t\le r\,}.
\]

For every such \(t\), the point
\[
(1,t,0)
\]
belongs to both \(V_1\) and \(V_3\), so this is also the range for a common point.

---

### Cross-sections on the plane \(y=t\)

Identify the plane \(y=t\) with the \((x,z)\)-plane. Put
\[
\alpha=\sqrt{r^2-t^2},\qquad \beta=\sqrt{r^2-(t-1)^2}.
\]
For \(t\in[1-r,r]\), both \(\alpha,\beta\ge 0\).

The section \(V_1\cap\{y=t\}\) is a horizontal stadium whose axis is the segment
\[
(x,z)=(0,0)\ \text{to}\ (1,0),
\]
with radius \(\alpha\):
\[
V_1\cap\{y=t\}
=
\bigl([0,1]\times[-\alpha,\alpha]\bigr)
\cup D_2((0,0),\alpha)\cup D_2((1,0),\alpha),
\]
where \(D_2(P,\rho)\) is a disk in the \((x,z)\)-plane.

The section \(V_3\cap\{y=t\}\) is a vertical stadium whose axis is the segment
\[
(x,z)=(1,0)\ \text{to}\ (1,1),
\]
with radius \(\beta\):
\[
V_3\cap\{y=t\}
=
\bigl([1-\beta,1+\beta]\times[0,1]\bigr)
\cup D_2((1,0),\beta)\cup D_2((1,1),\beta).
\]

So in the same \((x,z)\)-plane:

- \(V_1\) gives a horizontal stadium along \(x\in[0,1]\);
- \(V_3\) gives a vertical stadium along \(x=1,\ z\in[0,1]\);
- both contain the point \((1,0)\).

Also, for later use, since \(t\in[1-r,r]\subset[0,1]\), the section
\[
V_2\cap\{y=t\}
\]
is simply the disk
\[
D_2((1,0),r)
\]
in the \((x,z)\)-plane.

---

## (2)

We need the condition for
\[
V_1\cap V_3\subset V_2.
\]

Let \(X=(x,t,z)\in V_1\cap V_3\). Since \(t\in[1-r,r]\subset[0,1]\), the distance from \(X\) to the segment \(AB\) is
\[
d(X,V_2)^2=(x-1)^2+z^2.
\]

We claim that points of \(V_1\cap V_3\) outside the quadrant
\[
x<1,\qquad z>0
\]
are already inside \(V_2\).

- If \(x\ge1\), then because \(X\in V_1\), the nearest endpoint \(A=(1,0,0)\) gives
  \[
  (x-1)^2+t^2+z^2\le r^2,
  \]
  so
  \[
  (x-1)^2+z^2\le r^2.
  \]
  Thus \(X\in V_2\).

- If \(z\le0\), then because \(X\in V_3\), the nearest endpoint \(B=(1,1,0)\) gives
  \[
  (x-1)^2+(t-1)^2+z^2\le r^2,
  \]
  so again
  \[
  (x-1)^2+z^2\le r^2.
  \]
  Thus \(X\in V_2\).

Therefore, a point can fail to be in \(V_2\) only when
\[
x<1,\quad z>0.
\]

For such \(x,z\), using the cross-sections found in (1), we have

\[
z\le \alpha=\sqrt{r^2-t^2},
\]
from \(X\in V_1\), and
\[
1-x\le \beta=\sqrt{r^2-(t-1)^2},
\]
from \(X\in V_3\).

Thus in the cross-section plane \(y=t\), the relevant part of \(V_1\cap V_3\) lies in the rectangle
\[
1-\beta\le x\le1,\qquad 0\le z\le\alpha.
\]

The point farthest from the center \((1,0)\) of the disk \(V_2\cap\{y=t\}\) is
\[
(x,z)=(1-\beta,\alpha).
\]
Its squared distance from \((1,0)\) is
\[
\beta^2+\alpha^2
=
\bigl(r^2-(t-1)^2\bigr)+\bigl(r^2-t^2\bigr)
=
2r^2-\{t^2+(1-t)^2\}.
\]

For inclusion \(V_1\cap V_3\subset V_2\), we need this quantity to be at most \(r^2\) for all \(t\in[1-r,r]\):
\[
2r^2-\{t^2+(1-t)^2\}\le r^2
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
t^2+(1-t)^2\ge r^2.
\]

Since \(r>\frac12\), the interval \([1-r,r]\) contains \(t=\frac12\). The minimum of
\[
t^2+(1-t)^2
\]
on \([0,1]\) occurs at \(t=\frac12\), where it equals
\[
\frac14+\frac14=\frac12.
\]
Hence the necessary and sufficient condition is
\[
r^2\le \frac12.
\]

Under the given assumption \(\frac12<r<1\), the answer is

\[
\boxed{\frac12<r\le \frac1{\sqrt2}}.
\]

---

## (3) Volume of \(V_1\)

The solid \(V_1\) is a capsule: a cylinder of radius \(r\) and length \(1\), together with two hemispherical caps at the endpoints \(O\) and \(A\). The two hemispheres make one full sphere.

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1)
=
\pi r^2+\frac43\pi r^3.
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Vol}(V_1)=\pi r^2+\frac43\pi r^3.}
\]

---

## (4) Volume of \(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3\) under the condition in (2)

Assume now

\[
\frac12<r\le \frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]

Then by part (2),
\[
V_1\cap V_3\subset V_2.
\]

Hence
\[
V_1\cap V_2\cap V_3=V_1\cap V_3.
\]

By inclusion-exclusion,

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
\sum_i \operatorname{Vol}(V_i)
-\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_2)
-\operatorname{Vol}(V_2\cap V_3)
-\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_3)
+\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_2\cap V_3).
\]

Because \(V_1\cap V_3=V_1\cap V_2\cap V_3\), the last two terms cancel, so

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
3\operatorname{Vol}(V_1)
-
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_2)
-
\operatorname{Vol}(V_2\cap V_3).
\]

The two adjacent intersections \(V_1\cap V_2\) and \(V_2\cap V_3\) are congruent, so we compute \(V_1\cap V_2\).

---

### Adjacent intersection \(V_1\cap V_2\)

Shift the common endpoint \(A\) to the origin in coordinates
\[
u=1-x,\qquad v=y,\qquad w=z.
\]
Then \(V_1\) is the \(r\)-neighborhood of the segment
\[
0\le u\le1,\quad v=0,\quad w=0,
\]
and \(V_2\) is the \(r\)-neighborhood of the segment
\[
u=0,\quad 0\le v\le1,\quad w=0.
\]

For a fixed \(w\), put
\[
\rho=\sqrt{r^2-w^2}.
\]

In the \((u,v)\)-plane, the cross-section of \(V_1\cap V_2\) is as follows.

- If \(u\ge0,\ v\ge0\), the two cylinder parts impose
  \[
  u\le\rho,\qquad v\le\rho,
  \]
  giving a square of side \(\rho\), area
  \[
  \rho^2.
  \]

- In each of the other three quadrants, one endpoint ball condition gives a quarter-disk of radius \(\rho\), total area
  \[
  3\cdot \frac{\pi}{4}\rho^2=\frac{3\pi}{4}\rho^2.
  \]

Thus the total cross-section area at height \(w\) is

\[
\left(1+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)(r^2-w^2).
\]

Integrating over \(w\in[-r,r]\),

\[
\int_{-r}^r (r^2-w^2)\,dw
=
2r^3-\frac{2r^3}{3}
=
\frac43r^3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cap V_2)
=
\left(1+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)\frac43r^3
=
\pi r^3+\frac43r^3.
\]

By symmetry,

\[
\operatorname{Vol}(V_2\cap V_3)
=
\pi r^3+\frac43r^3.
\]

Now compute the union:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
&=
3\left(\pi r^2+\frac43\pi r^3\right)
-2\left(\pi r^3+\frac43r^3\right)\\
&=
3\pi r^2+4\pi r^3
-2\pi r^3-\frac83r^3\\
&=
3\pi r^2+\left(2\pi-\frac83\right)r^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence, under the condition \(\frac12<r\le\frac1{\sqrt2}\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
3\pi r^2+\left(2\pi-\frac83\right)r^3
}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}(V_1\cup V_2\cup V_3)
=
3\pi r^2+\frac{6\pi-8}{3}r^3.
}
\]
